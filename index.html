The problem

On a financial advisory team at a national financial services firm, the partner's assistant, called the first chair, does the work that keeps the practice running. Most partners became partners without ever doing that job, so they often can't see where a first chair's day goes or why some tasks take as long as they do.

Built with the people who use it

I built Flowdesk with the first chairs. It tracks task ownership, deadlines and time, and gives each first chair a record of their hours by task that they can show their partner. Their feedback changed most of the interface and the way data is entered.

A view for partners

Date-range PDF reports let partners see their team's hours by task. Partners say it helps them understand work they never did themselves, and it lets them ask why a specific task took as long as it did.

The constraint

Flowdesk handles private client information, so every user gets a separate workspace, passwords are hashed, and each request checks the user's account and role before any data is returned.

Where it stands

I ran it with one team for two months. Their referrals brought in the next four, and every team that started is still using it. Logging time doesn't save first chairs time. What it changes is the conversation: workload discussions now start from a shared record. I built it with OpenAI Codex, and one volunteer contributor now helps me improve it.
